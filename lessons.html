A What is a Verb?

A verb is a word that expresses an action, a state of being, or an occurrence. It tells us what a person, animal, or thing does.

Verbs are also called action words because they describe what someone or something is doing.

Examples:
The girl runs in the playground.
The boy eats an apple.
The teacher writes on the board.
The baby sleeps peacefully.

In these sentences, runs, eats, writes, and sleeps are verbs because they tell us what the subject does.

B Learning Objectives

After studying this lesson, learners are expected to:

  • Define what a verb is.
  • Identify verbs in simple sentences.
  • Recognize different types of verbs.
  • Use verbs correctly in sentences.
  • Demonstrate actions using appropriate verbs.

C Different Types of Verbs

1. Action Verbs

Action verbs are words that show what a person, animal, or thing does. These actions can be physical or mental.

Examples: run, jump, dance, sing, write, think.

Ana dances beautifully.
The children play outside.
I think about my answer.

2. Linking Verbs

Linking verbs connect the subject to a word that describes or identifies it. They do not show physical action.

Common examples: am, is, are, was, were, become, seem.

She is happy.
They are students.
He was tired.

3. Helping Verbs

Helping verbs work with the main verb to express an action or condition more clearly.

Common examples: is, am, are, was, were, has, have, will, can.

She is reading a book.
They are playing basketball.
I will study tonight.
He can swim.

E Why Are Verbs Important?

Verbs are important because they:

  • Help us express actions and activities.
  • Make sentences complete and meaningful.
  • Help us communicate our thoughts and ideas.
  • Tell us what people, animals, and things do.
  • Make our stories and conversations more interesting.
Remember:

"Verbs are action words that make our sentences meaningful!"
